### TITLE
When Does the Counterpress Work? Spatial Predictors of Possession Regain and Transition Risk in Elite Soccer

### ABSTRACT
Counterpressing is a defining tactical paradigm in elite soccer, yet quantitative evaluations typically treat counterpressing as an unstructured aggregate event. We analyze the spatial geometry governing counterpressing outcomes across 21,616 distinct open-play episodes and 414 matches from seven professional competitions in StatsBomb 360 data. Using freeze frames captured at counterpress initiation, we evaluate spatial predictors of two distinct outcomes: controlled possession regain within 5 seconds (34.65% prevalence) and opponent dangerous transition within 15 seconds (22.41% prevalence).

Across 5-fold match-clustered cross-validation, event-level freeze-frame spatial geometry provided statistically reliable incremental predictive value over match context alone for 5-second regains (Model 1 Context ROC-AUC = 0.7926 vs Model 3 Full Spatial Logistic ROC-AUC = 0.8156; Delta ROC-AUC = +0.0230, paired bootstrap 95% CI [+0.0181, +0.0278]; Delta PR-AUC = +0.0377). In multivariable clustered logistic models, shorter nearest-teammate distance was strongly associated with higher regain odds (OR = 0.800 [95% CI: 0.729, 0.878] per 1 SD / 8.2 StatsBomb coordinate units), complemented by broader numerical advantages at 10 and 15 coordinate units (OR = 1.071 and 1.144).

Conversely, transition danger was dominated by pitch location: danger rates were nearly three times higher following defensive-third turnovers (31.57%) than attacking-third turnovers (11.28%; OR = 0.379 [95% CI: 0.359, 0.399]). Furthermore, central counterpresses carried significantly higher transition danger than wide counterpresses (25.15% vs 16.86%; OR = 1.219 [95% CI: 1.174, 1.266]). These results demonstrate that ball recovery is primarily a local compactness problem, whereas transition danger is governed by macro-level pitch geography.
